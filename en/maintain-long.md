# Practical Ways to Maintain Your Health Without Being Too Strict

*Maintaining Quality of Life While Living with a Medical History*

When you have experienced a serious illness in the past or need ongoing medical monitoring, it is easy to become overly concerned about your daily health and lifestyle.

There are many things we can do to maintain our health, such as exercising, eating well, getting enough sleep, checking blood pressure, and keeping track of physical symptoms. However, trying to do everything perfectly every day can gradually become stressful and difficult to maintain.

Maintaining good health is important, but spending every day worrying about health management is not the purpose of life.

What matters is developing a few simple habits that are easy to maintain, while allowing yourself to live comfortably and without unnecessary worry.

## 1. Don't Make Health Management a Burden

Keeping track of your physical condition and analyzing health data can be useful when necessary. However, there is no need to measure every possible indicator every day or become overly concerned about minor changes.

For example, frequent blood pressure measurements may not always be necessary unless your doctor has specifically recommended them.

Reviewing changes in your physical condition or looking back at symptoms from time to time can be useful. There is no need to turn health monitoring into a daily obligation.

Leaving yourself enough time and mental space to enjoy everyday life is also an important part of maintaining your quality of life.

## 2. Have Two Exercise Plans: A Regular Routine and an Easy-Day Option

*Keep Moving, Even on Low-Energy Days*

Activities such as walking are valuable because of the benefits they can provide when practiced regularly.

However, if you feel that you must always complete your usual walking route, exercising can become a burden on days when you feel tired or simply don't feel motivated.

One practical solution is to prepare two different options in advance.

- **Regular routine:** Walk your usual route.
- **Easy-day option:** Go outside and walk for just 15 minutes.

The important thing is not to complete a full workout every time, but to keep the habit of being physically active from disappearing.

When several days pass without exercise, doing nothing can gradually become the new normal.

Having a shorter alternative makes it easier to get moving, even when motivation is low.

Of course, this does not mean forcing yourself to exercise when you are unwell or when your doctor has advised you to rest. There is no need to push yourself at the expense of your health.

## 3. Rest Before You Feel Exhausted

*Rest Before You Run Out of Energy*

Rather than waiting until you feel exhausted, try to take breaks before fatigue builds up.

This is particularly important when you are living with an ongoing health concern. It helps to avoid pushing yourself based on how energetic you used to feel before your illness.

For example, you might lie down and rest for about 20 minutes before you become too tired. You do not necessarily need to fall asleep. Simply lying down quietly and allowing your body to relax can be enough.

Instead of trying to increase the amount you accomplish each day, prioritize maintaining a relatively stable physical condition by taking breaks when needed.

The idea is to think, "It's time to take a break while I still feel okay," rather than "I can keep going a little longer."

## 4. Be Mindful of Seasonal Changes

*Protect Yourself from Dry Air and Cold Weather*

During winter, dry air can irritate the throat and place additional strain on the respiratory system.

Simple precautions can help, such as maintaining appropriate indoor humidity, drinking water regularly, and keeping your neck warm when going outside.

If you have noticed that a cold throat or exposure to dry air tends to trigger respiratory discomfort, it is better to take precautions in advance rather than wait for symptoms to appear.

Understanding how your body responds to different environmental conditions can be a useful part of everyday health management.

## 5. Don't Underestimate Constipation

*Prepare Your Diet to Support Regular Bowel Movements*

If you tend to experience physical discomfort following constipation, it is important to pay attention to your digestive health as part of your everyday routine.

Preparing side dishes containing dietary fiber, such as vegetables, beans, and seaweed, can make it easier to maintain a balanced diet.

Having these foods ready in your refrigerator is especially helpful on busy days or when you do not feel like preparing a meal from scratch.

You do not need to prepare perfect meals every day. Simply keeping a supply of nutritious foods and ready-to-eat side dishes can make healthy eating much easier to maintain.

If constipation continues despite dietary adjustments, there is no need to rely solely on food and water. Consulting a doctor or pharmacist about appropriate medication is also a reasonable option.

Rather than dismissing constipation as a minor inconvenience, consider it one of the warning signs your body may be giving you.

## 6. Follow a Few Essential Rules and Relax About the Rest

*Keep the Essentials, Relax About the Rest*

It helps to establish a few basic rules for maintaining your health.

- Have both a regular exercise routine and a shorter alternative for low-energy days.
- Take breaks before you become exhausted.
- Protect yourself from seasonal dryness and cold weather.
- Prepare meals that include adequate dietary fiber.
- Pay attention to physical changes that may signal a decline in your condition, including constipation.
- Keep up with medical appointments and examinations recommended by your doctor.

Once these basic habits are in place, there is no need to worry about every little detail. You can simply relax and go about your daily life at a comfortable pace.

There is no need to aim for a perfect score every day.

Perhaps you took a short walk, rested before becoming too tired, or made a little effort to eat well. That is already something worth appreciating.

Maintaining good health is not about achieving quick results in a short period of time. It is about finding a sustainable way to look after yourself over the long term.

**Don't push yourself too hard. Avoid letting inactivity become a habit. And rest before you become exhausted.**

Especially when living with a history of illness, it is important not to let health management take over your life. Instead, learn to work with your body, maintain a comfortable daily rhythm, and leave yourself enough room to enjoy life.
