#### What Is the Definition of a Malignant Tumor?

*A malignant tumor is a type of tumor commonly referred to as cancer. It has the ability to grow by invading surrounding normal tissues and may spread to distant organs through the bloodstream or lymphatic system. This process is known as metastasis.*

However, the definition of a malignant tumor does not simply mean a tumor that grows larger or one that requires treatment.

### The Difference Between Benign and Malignant Tumors

Benign and malignant tumors differ in several important ways.

- **Growth pattern:** Benign tumors generally grow by pushing aside surrounding tissues, whereas malignant tumors can invade and grow into surrounding tissues.
- **Metastasis:** Benign tumors do not normally spread to distant organs, while malignant tumors may metastasize.
- **Impact on the body:** Depending on their location and size, tumors may impair the function of nearby organs or, in some cases, become life-threatening.

### Malignant Tumors of the Adrenal Glands

Tumors that develop in the adrenal glands can also be classified as benign or malignant.

However, imaging examinations alone, which assess the size and shape of a tumor, may not always be sufficient to determine whether an adrenal tumor is benign or malignant.

For example, in adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC), evidence of invasion into surrounding tissues or metastasis is an important basis for determining malignant behavior.

Similarly, an increase in tumor size or excessive hormone production does not, by itself, establish that a tumor is malignant.

### Regarding the Tumor in My Remaining Adrenal Gland

When a tumor containing fat is discovered in the remaining adrenal gland, the presence of fat can be a useful imaging feature when considering the possibility of a benign tumor.

However, the presence of fat alone cannot confirm that a tumor is benign. A comprehensive assessment is necessary, taking into account the type of tumor, imaging characteristics, changes over time, and hormone test results.

### A Diagnosis of Malignancy and the Need for Treatment Are Not the Same Thing

One important point is that determining whether a tumor is benign or malignant is not necessarily the same as determining whether treatment is required.

Even a benign tumor may require treatment if it causes excessive hormone production or compresses nearby organs.

On the other hand, treatment options for malignant tumors also vary depending on the tumor type and the extent of the disease.
