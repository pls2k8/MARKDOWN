
# How I Respond When I Sense I May Be Bowel on Waking

*When I Learned to Recognize a Difficult Day Before It Began*

## On Days When I Don’t Feel Well, I Decide It’s a “Low-Power Day”

When I wake up in the morning, there are times when I can tell that I clearly haven’t had enough sleep or that my body feels heavy.

In the past, I would push myself to get through my plans anyway. But I’ve learned that on days like these, starting out at a lower pace can help keep my condition from getting worse later.

First, I drink some water and keep breakfast light rather than forcing myself to eat a full meal. Then, early in the morning, I set aside 15 to 30 minutes to rest my body—closing my eyes or reducing the amount of stimulation around me.

If I’m still very sleepy, I also consider taking a nap. I keep it brief rather than sleeping for a long time.

The important thing is not to wait until my fatigue reaches its limit before resting. Instead, I treat the morning as a time to switch on a “yellow warning light.” I reduce my plans and activity for the day, and lower the demands on myself early while keeping an eye on how I feel.

On mornings when I realize I haven’t slept enough, I try to follow this pattern:

**Notice that I haven’t had enough sleep**  
↓  
**Reduce my plans**  
↓  
**Rest during the morning**  
↓  
**Reduce my activity even further in the afternoon**  
↓  
**Stop before my “battery runs out”**

Rather than trying to force myself back to normal, I lower my output early.

That gives me more room to recover during the day, before my condition has a chance to deteriorate further.
