<section id="section01">

<div class="idea-block">
  <h4 class="idea-title">01 | A 15-Minute Break with Your Eyes Closed</h4>
  <p class="idea-text">

How about taking a quiet 15-minute break at 10 a.m. and 3 p.m.?

All you need is an eye mask, which you can easily find at a dollar store or similar shop.

Even if the room is bright, closing your eyes creates a dark environment.

Simply lie down and breathe slowly.

You don't have to fall asleep. It's perfectly fine to just rest.

</p>
</div>

</section>

<section id="section02">

<div class="idea-block">
  <h4 class="idea-title">02 | Rest Before You Feel Tired</h4>
  <p class="idea-text">

Sometimes, taking a break a little earlier feels easier than waiting until you are already tired.

I have come to realize that the timing that once seemed perfectly fine when I was feeling well can change depending on my physical condition.

When fatigue carries over, my concentration and mood tend to become less stable.

Taking short breaks along the way can help me maintain a more consistent pace.

I continue to use this as a small daily adjustment rather than a major lifestyle change.

</p>
</div>

</section>

<section id="section03">

<div class="idea-block">
  <h4 class="idea-title">03 | Finding the Right Time to Read</h4>
  <p class="idea-text">

I often hear people say they stopped reading because their eyesight became worse.

I sometimes feel the same way.

When I started paying closer attention to myself, I noticed that reading became easier or more difficult depending on the time of day.

In the evening, I sometimes find that more fatigue has built up than I expected.

By experimenting with times when I feel less tired, I discovered that reading can become easier to maintain as a habit.

Perhaps the key is finding the time of day that works best for you.

</p>
</div>

</section>

<section id="section04">

<div class="idea-block">
  <h4 class="idea-title">04 | Have a Plan for Getting Home</h4>
  <p class="idea-text">

Before going out, I decide on at least one way to get home.

If my physical condition worsens, I keep the option of taking a taxi rather than pushing myself.

Simply having a ride-hailing app installed on my phone makes the decision a little easier.

During the ride, I close my eyes and use the time as a short rest.

I see this as a precaution to avoid reaching the point where I can no longer move while away from home.

</p>
</div>

</section>

<section id="section05">

<div class="idea-block">
  <h4 class="idea-title">05 | Why on Earth Did I Choose That? (Overseas Edition) – Part 1</h4>
  <p class="idea-text">

This happened when I was living overseas.

There was a moment when I started feeling that my physical energy was running a little low.

The obvious solution would have been to take a taxi and head straight home.

In fact, that would have been the simplest and most reliable option.

But for some reason, the idea that popped into my head was:

"Maybe I can recover if I raise my blood sugar."

The general idea didn't seem entirely unreasonable.

The problem was what happened next.

I gave some small change to a child nearby and asked them to buy me a Coca-Cola.

For some reason, I asked for two cans. One for me and the other as a mysterious reward for the child.

Looking back calmly, I should have simply taken a taxi. That would probably have solved everything.

But at the time, the idea that "a Coke might make everything better" seemed surprisingly practical.

I did feel a little more energetic afterward, but the overall situation didn't really improve.

Looking back, my reaction is:

"Well, that wasn't really the point."

It seems that when I get close to my limits, I sometimes think I'm looking for a solution but end up choosing the most roundabout way of dealing with the situation.

</p>
</div>

</section>

<section id="section18">

<div class="idea-block">
  <h4 class="idea-title">18 | Days When Palpitations and a Slight Feverish Feeling Occur Together</h4>
  <p class="idea-text">

In the afternoon, there are days when I experience palpitations together with a slight feverish feeling.

A feeling of heaviness develops around the center of my forehead, and my headache gradually becomes more uncomfortable.

I have noticed that this tends to happen more often on days when I have pushed myself too hard in the morning or haven't been able to take breaks while out.

If I continue pushing myself, the discomfort can become even worse.

When I sense these symptoms coming on, I try to move to a quiet place whenever possible.

I reduce the time I spend looking at my phone or other screens, and try to limit conversations and incoming information.

I also take slow, deep breaths to help settle my breathing.

These are not major measures, but sometimes they help prevent things from getting worse.

</p>
</div>
